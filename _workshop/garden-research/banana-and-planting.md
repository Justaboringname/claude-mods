# The banana plant (芭蕉, *Musa basjoo*) and the planting of Suzhou gardens: notes for the illustrator

Researched 2026-11-16. Covers: what the plant looks like, why the current drawing reads as a palm, how Chinese painters draw it, where Suzhou gardens plant it, the Suzhou plant palette, and an honest note on palms. Sources are listed with URLs at the end.

## In three plain sentences

1. A 芭蕉 is a clump of two or three short, fat, green "trunks" (really rolled-up leaf bases). Each carries a few huge paddle-shaped leaves on short stout stalks. The leaves arch over and droop, with a rolled new leaf standing up in the middle like a candle.
2. A drawing reads as a palm when the leaves come out of one point on top of a thin bare trunk, or when the leaves are narrow, stiff and pointed, or when they are combed into many even strips. A banana leaf is one wide, blunt blade with a thick midrib, and it is torn only here and there.
3. In Suzhou the plant sits against a white wall, in a wall corner, outside a window or beside a Taihu rock, usually with bamboo. It is drawn in grey-green ink washes, not bright green.

## 1. The whole plant: form and proportions

### 1.1 The clump
- **Number of stems.** Basjoo makes a clump from suckers. The Ming compilation 格物總論 says 「芭蕉叢生，根出地面，兩三莖成一簇」 ("grows in clumps, two or three stems to a cluster"). Draw **2–3 pseudostems of clearly unequal height** from one spot: tallest = 1, second ≈ 0.6–0.75, a young sucker ≈ 0.25–0.4.
  - A modern painting tutorial places two plants off-centre, "one taller and darker, the other lighter".
  - The 听雨轩 description speaks of 「一丛芭蕉」 / 「一本芭蕉」 behind the hall (one clump, one plant).
- **Height and spread.**
  - Wikipedia gives pseudostems of about 2–2.5 m.
  - NC State Extension gives the whole plant as 6–14 ft tall and 6–14 ft wide, so **crown spread ≈ plant height**.
  - In Suzhou the literati wanted it kept **short**. 長物志 (by 文震亨, a Suzhou man): 「但取短者為佳，蓋高則葉為風所碎耳」 ("only the short ones are good, for when tall the leaves are shredded by the wind").
  - So keep the clump **below the top of the garden wall**, about head-to-eave height, not tree-like.
- **Winter.** In Suzhou it dies back in winter. 閩部疏 says 「吳中蕉三月始抽萌」 (in the Wu region the banana first sprouts in the 3rd month). Full leaves therefore mean late spring to autumn.

### 1.2 The pseudostem (假茎)
- **What it is.** Not wood. ProMusa calls it "tightly packed overlapping leaf sheaths", spirally arranged and "very fleshy". The 群芳譜 says 「其莖軟重，皮相裹，外微青，裏白」 (soft and heavy, skins wrapped round each other, pale green outside, white inside).
- **Silhouette.** A stout column, slightly swollen at the foot and tapering a little to the crown (crown width ≈ 0.6–0.7 × base width).
  - For a 240-px plant make it about **10–16 px wide at the base**.
  - Never a pencil-thin pole, and never a tall bare trunk. The pseudostem is roughly **0.45–0.55 of total plant height**. The leaves are as long as, or longer than, the visible stem.
- **Texture.** This is what the Leiden and Vienna photos show.
  - 2–4 long, faint **vertical lines** where the sheaths overlap, with occasional slight steps where a sheath edge ends.
  - A few irregular **dark brown blotches** on the green.
  - At the base, **dry brown sheath shreds** peeling outward and lying on the ground.
  - **No rings, no diamond pattern, no hairy fibre.** Those are palm trunks.

### 1.3 How the leaves leave the stem
- **Arrangement.** Leaves are spirally arranged and crowded at the top (Flora of Taiwan for *Musa*: "Leaves spirally arranged, crowded"; ProMusa: sheaths "spirally arranged").
  - In side view, successive leaves go left, right, toward you and away from you.
  - They attach at **staggered heights over the top 10–15 % of the stem**. That is never a single point; staggered attachment is the strongest anti-palm cue.
- **Petiole (叶柄).** Short and stout: "Petiole to 30 cm, robust" on a 2–3 m blade, so about **1/7–1/10 of blade length**. It is channelled on top, and per ProMusa the sheath "contracts into a petiole" which "continues… becoming the mid rib". Draw the petiole flowing straight into the midrib with no joint.
- **Angle.**
  - The **1–2 youngest leaves** stand nearly upright, within about 15–20° of vertical.
  - Mature leaves leave the stem at **20–45° from vertical**, rise, then **arch over**. The outer half to two-thirds droops, so the tip of a mature leaf is level with or below its attachment point.
  - 蘇轍: 「攲傾大葉不勝肥」 ("the big leaves lean over, too heavy to hold up").
  - The overall crown is an **asymmetric vase or fountain**, not a sphere or starburst.

### 1.4 The blade (叶片)
- **Shape.** Oblong paddle, "paddle-shaped, oblong" (NCSU); Chinese descriptions say 長圓形, 先端鈍 (oblong, blunt apex). The sides run nearly parallel for the middle ~60 %.
  - **Base:** "rounded and asymmetrical". One side starts a little lower on the petiole than the other.
  - **Tip:** blunt and rounded, often slightly lopsided. Never a long tapering point.
- **Length : width.** Draw **≈ 3 : 1**; the acceptable range is 2.5–4 : 1.
  - Wikipedia gives up to 2 m × 70 cm (≈ 2.9:1); NCSU gives 6 ft × 2 ft (3:1). The photos and the 孙克弘 album leaf look about the same.
  - The FRPS-style text (maigoo, and a WFO-style database) says 2–3 m × 25–30 cm, which would be about 8–10:1. That disagrees with every photo, so **ignore it**. A narrow blade is exactly what drifts toward a palm leaflet.
  - Old Chinese texts give 「葉長一丈，或七八尺，廣尺餘」 / 「廣一尺至二尺」, roughly 5–10:1. These are literary estimates of southern bananas, so don't use them.
- **Midrib (主脉).**
  - Thick and pale, the strongest line in the leaf.
  - Draw it about 1.5–2.5 × the outline weight at the base, tapering to the tip, and **continuous from petiole to tip**.
  - When the blade is V-folded along the midrib (common), the midrib becomes one edge of the visible shape.
- **Side veins (侧脉).**
  - Very many, parallel and unbranched. ProMusa: they run "parallel to each other in a long S shape from midrib to margin" and "do not branch, which results in leaves tearing easily".
  - **What the photos show:** veins leave the midrib at about **60–75°, slanting toward the tip**, then sweep a little further toward the tip at the margin.
  - At 150–300 px, **draw only 4–8 per visible half-blade**:
    - thin (about half the outline weight) and pale
    - slightly curved
    - unevenly spaced, with some stretches left blank
    - **stopping short of the margin**
  - Painting tutorials insist that veins should not be mechanically parallel or crossing (「叶脉不能平行交叉」).
  - 路德延's line 「葉如斜界紙」 ("a leaf like paper ruled with slanting lines") captures the look.
- **Underside.** Paler. A twisted leaf that shows its underside takes a lighter wash.

### 1.5 Wind tears (破叶)
- **How they form.**
  - The veins are parallel and unbranched, so the blade tears easily along them (ProMusa).
  - "The large leaves are very easily torn by the wind" (temperate.theferns.info).
  - Torn strips reduce heat stress (Taylor & Sexton 1972, *Ecology*).
- **What a torn leaf looks like.**
  - Slits run **from the margin inward along the vein direction**, so they slant like the veins (60–75° to the midrib), not straight across.
  - They reach part or all of the way to the midrib but **never cross it**. The midrib stays whole and every strip hangs from it, so it is still **one leaf**.
  - Strips are **unequal**: wide intact panels next to a couple of narrow ribbons.
  - Old outer leaves are torn more; young leaves are intact.
  - In Chinese verse: 吳寬 on a 沈周 painting, 「破葉留故綠」 ("torn leaves keep their old green"); 長物志: 「葉為風所碎」.
- **For the drawing.** Tear only **1–2 older leaves**, with **1–3 slits each**. Leave the outer margin continuous between the slits, and let a freed strip drop 1–3 px out of line. A whole blade combed into even strips is a **feather palm**.

### 1.6 The rolled new leaf (卷心, "cigar leaf")
- Each new leaf "emerges from the centre of the pseudostem as a rolled cylinder", "tightly coiled, whitish" (ProMusa).
- Chinese poets saw it as a green candle or a rolled letter:
  - 錢珝《未展芭蕉》: 「冷燭無煙綠蠟乾，芳心猶卷怯春寒」 ("a cold candle, smokeless, of dry green wax; its heart still rolled, afraid of spring cold")
  - 路德延: 「心似倒抽書」
- **Draw it** at the centre top of the tallest stem:
  - a slender upright **spindle**, about 1/3–1/2 of a blade length tall and about 1/5–1/6 of a blade width wide
  - tapering to a point, with one spiral seam line
  - in a paler wash than the leaves
- 齊白石 once declined to paint a rolled banana leaf (for 老舍's line 「芭蕉葉卷抱秋花」) because he could not remember which way it rolls. At this size, one spiral seam is enough; don't worry about the direction.

### 1.7 Old leaves
- Banana leaves wither on the plant rather than falling. 陸佃《埤雅》 via 本草綱目: 「蕉不落葉，一葉舒則一葉焦」 ("bananas do not drop leaves; as one unfurls, another scorches"), hence the name 蕉.
- 蘇轍: 「狼藉秋霜脫敝衣」 ("in autumn frost it sheds tattered clothes").
- **Draw 1–2 dead leaves hanging down against the stem**: folded, frayed, tips toward the ground, in muted ochre-brown.

## 2. Why the drawing reads as a palm, and how to avoid it

The Vienna photo of a Chinese windmill palm (*Trachycarpus fortunei*, 棕榈) standing behind a stand of *Musa basjoo* (Commons, Hirschstetten 2014) shows the contrast directly.

| Feature | 芭蕉 banana (draw this) | Fan palm, e.g. 棕榈 *Trachycarpus* (avoid) | Feather palm, e.g. coconut or date (avoid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stem | Short, stout green column of sheaths, about half the plant height; leaves start right at its top; old leaves hang along it; 2–3 stems in a clump | Single tall trunk (to 12–20 m), wrapped in coarse brown hairy fibre (棕衣), bare below the crown | Tall bare trunk with rings or leaf-base scars; crown only at the top |
| Leaf attachment | Short stout petiole (≈1/7–1/10 of the blade) at staggered heights in a spiral | Long thin spiny petiole (60–100 cm); all leaves radiate from the trunk top | Long rachis; crown of fronds from one point |
| Leaf | **One** continuous oblong blade, ≈3:1, blunt rounded tip | Round **fan** of 20–60 narrow segments radiating from **one point** at the end of the petiole, split halfway or more | **Many equal narrow leaflets** evenly spaced along both sides of a rib; frond tapers to a point |
| Lines inside the outline | Thick midrib plus fine pale veins **inside** a closed margin | Pleats or folds radiating from one point | The "lines" are separate leaflets with gaps between them; there is no outer margin |
| Posture | Arching, drooping, twisting; asymmetric vase shape | Stiff fans, tips of segments drooping; ball-shaped crown | Fronds arch, but as a comb; starburst crown |
| Tears | A few irregular slits on old leaves only | Not applicable; the segments are its normal form | Not applicable; the leaflets are its normal form |

**Likeliest causes of the current "palm" read**, in order:
1. Leaves fanning out from one point at the top of a thin stem.
2. Narrow, pointed, stiff blades.
3. Too many, too even tears or vein strokes, which turn each blade into a comb.

Fix all three.

## 3. How Chinese painters draw 芭蕉 (what was looked at)

- **孙克弘, 蕉石 album leaf (明清諸家集錦冊, Palace Museum).** Closest model for a fine-line SVG.
  - Ink outline only. One short, slightly flaring pseudostem with leaves fountaining out of its top.
  - Each blade is two curved outline strokes plus a midrib, with short slanted vein ticks (about 8–12 per half).
  - Small nicks in some margins stand for tears. One upright rolled leaf at the centre.
  - A Taihu-type rock with moss dots on the left, bamboo at the foot, dotted ground.
- **李鱓, Banana Plant by a Garden Rock (Yale).**
  - Tall clump to one side of a tall upright rock; long outlined blades with a strong midrib and slanting veins.
  - Pale colour washes (blue-grey, faint red-brown), grasses at the base.
- **徐渭, 蕉石牡丹圖 (Shanghai Museum).**
  - Pure ink. Blades are big wet grey washes with ragged, feathered lower edges hanging from tall pale stems, behind a huge dark rock.
  - Proves that ink alone reads as banana when the masses are **wide and drooping**.
- **陳衡恪, 蕉石幽鳥 (Palace Museum).**
  - Wash blades in pale grey-green and pale brown with dark vein strokes laid over the wash.
  - The new leaf is a rolled cone with spiral bands; rock at the base.
- **錢榖 (Suzhou school, pupil of 文徵明), Scholar Reading under Banana Trees (Princeton).**
  - Banana grove behind a dark, holed Taihu rock, in muted ink with pale blue-green.
  - The Suzhou version of the 蕉石 motif.
- **Tutorial method (modern 写意 lessons).** Two techniques:
  - 写叶法: a broad side-brush stroke per half-leaf, then vein strokes with the tip while still wet.
  - 勾叶法: first the midrib in pale ink to fix the leaf's gesture, then the outline, then the side veins; then a pale wash leaving the midrib white.
  - Colour is 花青 (indigo) mixed with 藤黄 (gamboge) "with only a slight green tint".
  - The hardest part is the leaf's turn (「芭蕉叶最难的是理解它的翻转」): a slight change in line flips a leaf to show its other face.
- **Literary images** to keep in mind:
  - 「葉如斜界紙」: the slanting veins
  - 「心似倒抽書」 and 「綠蠟」: the rolled heart
  - 「破葉留故綠」: tears
  - 「一葉舒則一葉焦」: dead leaves
  - 徐寅: 「閒倚東牆卓翠旗」, "idly leaning on the east wall like a raised green banner". This one is the planting position, too.
- **Not confirmed.** I did not view 八大山人's or 齊白石's own banana paintings. Only the 齊白石 anecdote above is sourced.

## 4. Where Suzhou gardens plant it, and with what

- **Official description (Suzhou Garden Bureau).**
  - 「其中芭蕉多植于庭院，窗前或墙隅，姿态扶疏，绿荫如盖」: banana is mostly planted in courtyards, in front of windows or in wall corners.
  - Small courtyards use a white wall as the background, sometimes with 「玲珑剔透的湖石」 (pierced Taihu rock), so that shadows play on the wall. Common plants there are 竹、天竹、蜡梅、山茶、海棠、**芭蕉**.
  - At open and lattice windows the planting is limited to 「小枝横斜，一叶芭蕉，或几竿修竹，半掩窗扉，若隐若现」: a slanting twig, **a single banana leaf**, or a few bamboo canes half-covering the window.
  - On raised rock-edged flower beds (花台), banana is listed among the herbaceous plants with 芍药、萱草、玉簪、书带草 and others.
- **听雨轩, Humble Administrator's Garden.** A self-contained small courtyard east of 嘉实亭.
  - In front of the hall: a small pool with a few lotus leaves, with banana and green bamboo at the pool edge.
  - Behind the hall: a clump of banana.
  - The arrangement makes rain sound different on each plant (新浪旅游 guide text).
- **Original Humble Administrator's Garden (文徵明, 1533 and 1551 albums).** One of its scenes was **芭蕉槛** (the Banana Railing): 「芭蕉槛在槐雨亭之左」.
  - The poem: 「新蕉十尺强，得雨净如沐。**不嫌粉堵高，雅称朱栏曲**。秋声入枕凉，晓色分窗绿……」 ("new bananas over ten feet, washed clean by rain; they don't mind the tall **white wall**, and suit the curving red railing well… dawn colour splits green across the window").
  - The canonical setting is banana + white wall + railing + window.
- **Literati prescriptions.**
  - 李漁《閑情偶寄》: 「幽齋但有隙地，即宜種蕉」 (wherever a quiet studio has a gap of ground, plant banana). It shades pavilions and windows green: 「能使臺榭軒窗盡染碧色」.
  - 群芳譜: 「書窗左右不可無此君」 (the study window must have it on either side).
  - 長物志: 「綠牕分映」 (it should share its green with the window) and keep it short.
- **Banana with rock (蕉石).**
  - 高啟 (a Suzhou poet): 「叢蕉倚孤石」 ("a clump of banana leaning on a lone rock").
  - 張綖: 「太湖石畔新涼院」.
  - 顧璘《蕉石亭》: 「怪石如筆格，上植蕉葉青」.
  - A 2003 study of *Musa* in Jiangnan (Suzhou) gardens: often planted with **strange rocks, 梧桐 (*Firmiana*) and bamboo**, at "garden gates, near windows, under eaves and corridors, beside railings, near steps, along streams".
  - A garden-design study lists 「蕉竹相配」 (banana with bamboo) among the classic Suzhou pairings. 听雨轩 also pairs them.
  - A 2025 Xinhua photo guide to 网师园 suggests framing "a lattice window enclosing banana and Taihu rock".
- **In the moon-gate scene.** Put the clump against the wall beside the gate and let one leaf arch across the edge of the opening. This is the same 「一叶芭蕉，半掩窗扉」 idea, applied to the gate.
- **Foot of the plant.** Hide the base with 书带草 (lilyturf, *Ophiopogon*) or a low rock, plus a few moss dots. The bureau lists 书带草 with 芭蕉 among the plants for rock-edged flower beds. Using a tuft at the foot is my suggestion, matching the grass at the foot in the 李鱓 and 钱榖 paintings.
- **园冶.** 「夜雨芭蕉，似杂鲛人之泣泪」 (园说) and 「窗虚蕉影玲珑」 (城市地) are widely quoted as 园冶. I could not open a primary text to verify them, so treat them as attributions.

## 5. The Suzhou garden plant palette (official list) and the palm question

**Palette.** From the Suzhou Garden Bureau page, which follows the classic survey of Suzhou gardens. The basic tone is deciduous trees with some evergreens, "辅以藤萝、竹类、芭蕉、草花" (supplemented by vines, bamboo, banana and flowers).

| Flowering, deciduous | 牡丹、玉兰、梅、桃、杏、李、海棠、紫薇、丁香、木槿、木芙蓉、辛夷、蜡梅、紫荆、绣球、迎春、连翘、棣棠… |

| Shade and forest trees | 罗汉松、白皮松、黑松、马尾松、桧柏、香樟; 梧桐、银杏、榆、榉、朴、槐、枫杨、合欢… |

| Water | 荷花、睡莲、芦苇 |

**Palms: an honest note. The person's premise is not literally right.**
- The Suzhou Garden Bureau lists **棕榈** (*Trachycarpus fortunei*, Chinese windmill palm) among the evergreen foliage plants used in Suzhou gardens.
- 文震亨《長物志》 (Suzhou, late Ming), in its 芭蕉 entry: 「又作盆玩者，更可笑，不如㯶櫚為雅」. A potted banana is laughable, and the **palm is more elegant**. The same book also discusses 棕竹 (*Rhapis*, a small palm) for pots.
- Some modern transcriptions of 文徵明's 芭蕉槛 note add 「更植椶阴，宜为暑月」 ("palms were also planted for shade, suited to summer"). These are 北京日报 2026 and 木直艺术. The 珊瑚網 text and a sohu translation lack the clause, so treat it as uncertain.
- 棕榈 is native south of the Qinling, from the Yangtze region southward (zh.wikipedia), and is a common ornamental.
- **Conclusion:** palms do occur in the Suzhou garden tradition as a minor foliage plant, not a signature one. The person's real complaint, that the banana *looks like* a palm, stands. **The drawing should contain nothing palm-like**, and no palm should be added.

## 6. Colour: ink-wash, low saturation

This answers the person's third point. Painters use ink with 花青 + 藤黄 "略带绿意" (just a hint of green); 徐渭 uses ink alone. Suggested SVG fills for a light, paper-like ground; keep the hues and lower the lightness on a dark dusk ground.

| Element | Colour | HSL saturation and lightness |
|---|---|---|
| Front leaf wash | `#8E9A88` | S 8 %, L 56 % |
| Back or second-stem leaves | `#A9AF9C` or `#B8C0B0` | S 10–11 %, L 64–72 % |
| Dark inner leaves | `#6F7C6C` | S 6 % |
| Outline and midrib ink | `#3B3F3A` | 0.7–0.9 opacity |
| Veins | same ink | 0.35–0.5 opacity |
| Dead leaves and base shreds | `#A39274` (pale 赭石 ochre) | S 20 % |
| Stem blotches | `#6E6A5C` | |
| Rolled new leaf | `#B8C0B0` | |

Rules:
- No colour above about 20 % saturation.
- Let line and value carry the form, not hue.
- Front leaves are darker and back ones paler (the tutorial's "one darker, one lighter").

## 7. Size recipe for a plant about 240 px tall

| Part | Size |
|---|---|
| Main pseudostem | 110–130 px tall; 12–15 px wide at the foot, 8–10 px at the crown |
| Second stem | ~160 px total height, set behind and to one side, lighter |
| Sucker | ~70 px, 2 small upright leaves |
| Main stem's leaves | 5–7 plus 1 rolled leaf |
| Second stem's leaves | 3–4 |
| Blade length L | 100–140 px |
| Blade width | ≈ L/3 (35–45 px) |
| Petiole | 12–18 px |
| Midrib | ~2 px at the base tapering to ~0.8 px; outline ~1 px |
| Veins | 4–8 per half-blade, 0.5 px |
| Tears | 2 old leaves, 1–3 slits each |
| Rolled leaf | 45–60 px tall, 6–8 px wide |
| Dead leaves | 1–2 hanging, 60–90 px long |
| Rock | top at 0.3–0.6 of plant height, beside or in front of the foot |
| Ground | 6–9 Ophiopogon strokes, 10–20 px long, at the foot |

**Draw order** (the 勾叶法 sequence):
1. Stems.
2. Midribs, to fix each leaf's gesture.
3. Outlines.
4. Veins.
5. Washes, leaving the midrib pale.

## Draw it like this

1. Draw 2–3 pseudostems of clearly unequal height from one spot (≈1, 0.6–0.75, 0.3). Make the shorter ones paler and set behind, and keep the whole clump below the top of the wall.
2. Make each pseudostem a stout, slightly tapering column, swollen at the foot (about 1/15–1/25 of plant height wide). Give it 2–4 faint vertical sheath lines, a few dark blotches and 2–4 dry brown shreds peeling at the base.
3. Attach leaves only on the top 10–15 % of the stem, at staggered heights, alternating left, right, front and back in a spiral. Never fan them out of one point.
4. Give every leaf a short stout petiole (≈1/7–1/10 of the blade) that flows straight into a thick, pale midrib running unbroken to the tip.
5. Make each blade one closed oblong paddle about 3× as long as wide (2.5–4×), with near-parallel sides, a rounded lopsided base and a blunt rounded tip.
6. Arch the leaves. The 1–2 youngest stand nearly upright. Mature ones rise 20–45° from vertical and droop over so the outer half hangs. Twist or fold one or two so a half-blade or the paler underside shows.
7. Add veins sparingly: 4–8 thin, pale, slightly S-curved strokes per visible half, leaving the midrib at 60–75° toward the tip, unevenly spaced, stopping short of the margin.
8. Tear only 1–2 older leaves, with 1–3 irregular slits each, cut along the vein slant from the margin toward (never across) the midrib. Keep the margin continuous between slits and the strips unequal.
9. Stand one rolled new leaf upright at the centre of the tallest stem: a pale slender spindle about 1/3–1/2 of a blade long, with one spiral seam.
10. Hang 1–2 dead leaves down against the stem, folded and frayed, in muted ochre.
11. Wash in grey-green ink with saturation no higher than about 12 % (ochre ≤ 20 %): darker in front, paler behind. Let outline and midrib carry the drawing, as in 孙克弘's album leaf.
12. Set it as Suzhou does: against a white wall or in a wall corner, beside a Taihu rock (rock top at 0.3–0.6 of plant height), with a few bamboo canes and a tuft of 书带草 hiding the foot. At a window, let a single leaf half-cover the opening.

## Tell-tale mistakes

- **Leaves radiating from one point on top of a stem** reads as a fan palm, yucca or cordyline. Fix: staggered spiral attachment over the top of the stem, each leaf on its own stout petiole.
- **A blade split along its whole length into many even strips, or a row of narrow leaflets either side of a rib**, reads as a feather palm (coconut or date). Fix: one continuous blade with a closed margin, and at most 1–3 irregular slits on at most two old leaves.
- **Narrow strap or lance leaves (more than about 5:1) with pointed tips** read as palm leaflets, canna, iris or cordyline. Fix: a ≈3:1 oblong with near-parallel sides and a blunt rounded tip.
- **A tall, thin, bare trunk with a tuft on top** reads as a palm tree. Fix: a short, stout pseudostem about half the plant height, leaves starting right at its top, dead leaves hanging along it, and 2–3 unequal stems.
- **Rings, diamond hatching or hairy fibre on the trunk** reads as a palm (coconut rings, the 棕衣 fibre of 棕榈). Fix: a smooth column with long vertical sheath lines, a few blotches, and dry shreds only at the foot.
- **Stiff, straight leaves sticking out** read as palm or yucca. Fix: every mature leaf arches and droops at its outer half; twist or fold one or two.
- **A symmetrical starburst or ball-shaped crown** reads as a palm. Fix: an asymmetric vase or fountain with more weight to one side and one or two foreshortened leaves.
- **Vein lines that are dark, long, or run past the margin** read as leaflets of a feather palm. Fix: few thin, pale veins inside a closed outline that stop before the edge; the midrib is the darkest line.
- **Veins evenly spaced at exactly 90°** look like a botanical diagram, not ink painting. Fix: a 60–75° slant toward the tip, a slight S-curve, uneven gaps, and some stretches left blank.
- **A lone plant in open ground like a tree** reads as tropical scenery, not a Suzhou courtyard. Fix: tuck it against a white wall, a wall corner or a window, with a Taihu rock and bamboo, and keep it short (長物志: 「但取短者為佳」).
- **Saturated green, blue-green or yellow-green** loses the ink-wash Suzhou feeling. Fix: grey-green washes at 12 % saturation or less, ochre for dead leaves, and ink for lines.
- **Adding a palm because "palms do grow in Suzhou"**: the person explicitly does not want it, and it is not a signature plant. Fix: use bamboo, plum, osmanthus, crabapple or 梧桐 as companions instead.
