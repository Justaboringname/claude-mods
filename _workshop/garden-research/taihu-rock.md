# Taihu rock (太湖石) in the Suzhou garden — research notes for an SVG ink redraw

Researched 2026-11-16. Written for the illustrator who will redraw the rock as an ink line drawing with soft washes, about 150 to 300 px tall: in the moon-gate scene (inside the round gate) and in the pavilion scene (on the left among the lotus leaves, and as the rockery under the pavilion).

**In three sentences.** A Suzhou Taihu peak is a tall, water-worn grey limestone column that is **wider at the top than at the bottom**. It leans or hooks its "head" to one side and is pierced by a few holes of very different sizes, with the largest one high up. It stands alone on a low planted mound or a flat base stone, often against a white wall. It is not a symmetric, evenly holed blob sitting on a pedestal. A rockery (假山) is a different object: many such stones stacked in uneven layers, with caves and a ragged skyline, not a row of equal bumps.

Evidence labels used below: **[measured]** comes from a published dimension; **[photo-est.]** is my estimate from photographs listed in the sources; **[primary]** is a classical text read directly; **[secondary]** is a classical quote I saw only inside a modern article.

---

## 1. What the stone is: material, colour, surface

- **Material and origin.** Water-eroded limestone from the lake around Dongting (洞庭) Mountain near Suzhou. Ji Cheng's 园冶 (1631), 选石·太湖石 **[primary]**:
  - It is 性坚而润，有嵌空、穿眼、婉转、崄怪势: hard and smooth, with hollows, pierced eyes, twisting turns and a "dangerous, strange" posture.
  - 其质文理纵横，笼络起隐，于石面遍多坎……谓之「弹子窝」: its grain runs every way, net-like veins rise and sink, and the face is covered in pits called "bullet pits".
  - 此石以高大为贵: tall and large is what is valued.
  - The same wording is in the Song 云林石谱 (Du Wan, c.1133), which adds that the pits 扣之微有声 (ring faintly when tapped).
- **Colour.** 云林石谱 lists three kinds: white, blue-black (青黑) and pale blue-grey (微青 / 青灰). Modern summaries say the same: 白太湖石、青黑太湖石、青灰色太湖石. In every photo I examined the stone is **light to mid neutral grey**, sometimes chalky white, with darker grey stains in the hollows. It is never brown, ochre or green; the green in photos comes from moss and plants. This matters for the user's "too saturated" complaint: the stone should be **paper white plus grey ink, nothing else**.
- **Surface.**
  - The faces are rounded and water-smoothed.
  - Lean peaks are fluted lengthwise: 冠云峰 shows long vertical grooves.
  - Many faces carry shallow round pits (弹子窝 / 弹窝). Fan Chengda describes them as 鳞鳞作靥 (scaly dimples) and calls them water marks.
  - Edges are rounded rims, not sharp crystal facets. Sharp, blocky, layered facets belong to yellowstone (黄石) or Ying stone (英石), not Taihu.
- **Character.** The 1998 paper on garden peak stones (卢山 et al.) assigns 清, a soft yin quality, to 湖石, and the opposite qualities 顽/拙 (stubborn, blunt) to yellowstone. Visually: soft, curling, cloud-like Taihu against blocky, angular yellowstone.

## 2. The classical criteria and what each one looks like

The usual set is 瘦 皱 漏 透 ("thin, wrinkled, leaking, penetrating"), credited by tradition to Mi Fu and quoted by Zheng Banqiao. Su Shi and Zheng Banqiao add 丑 (ugly / strange); the full Su Shi phrase is 石丑而文. Later writers add 清 顽 拙. The meanings below come from Li Yu's 闲情偶寄 (1671) **[secondary]** and the 卢山 1998 paper **[specialist article]**.

| Term | What sources say | What to draw |
|---|---|---|
| **瘦** (lean) | 李渔: 壁立当空，孤峙无倚: stands like a wall in empty air, alone, leaning on nothing. 卢山: 孤崎无倚，当空兀立, slender like a standing beauty. The Lingering Garden's three peaks (冠云峰, 瑞云峰, 岫云峰) are the stock example. | A tall, narrow, standing column, clearly taller than wide (3:1 to 4:1 for this type), with sky on both sides. Not a thin flat plate: one popular saying is "宁玩圆蛋蛋，不要薄片片". |
| **皱** (wrinkled) | 卢山: 石面凹凸，隐脉交叉，石纹富于变化, an undulating face where hidden veins cross. 沈宗骞: 皴者，皱也. A painter's texture stroke *is* the stone's wrinkle. | Ridges and grooves that follow the mass, and lines that appear and vanish. Draw a few form-following strokes, not an all-over pattern. |
| **漏** (leaking) | 李渔: 石上有眼，四面玲珑. 卢山: mostly **vertical** passages, top to bottom. | Holes you can see from every side, some running downward through the stone. |
| **透** (penetrating) | 李渔: 此通于彼，彼通于此，若有道路可行: one opening leads to another as if there were a path. 卢山: mostly **horizontal** see-through openings. | At least one hole you can see *through* (sky or wall shows inside it), with hollows that link up. |
| **丑** (strange) | 卢山: 奇突之美; ugliness taken to the extreme becomes beauty. | Irregular and unbalanced-looking, with no symmetry and nothing "pretty" or vase-like. |
| **清** (clear, soft yin) | 卢山: 阴柔，多指太湖石. | Soft curling lobes and a light grey tone; not heavy and black. |

Sources disagree on 透 versus 漏. Some swap the two, and one says 透 is about clarity of texture. For drawing it does not matter: the stone needs both through-holes and blind hollows.

## 4. How a single peak (立峰) is set up

- **Ji Cheng's rule for a peak [primary, 园冶·掇山·峰]:** 峰石一块者，相形何状，选合峰纹石，令匠凿笋为座，理宜上大下小，立之可观. A one-piece peak has a tenon cut into its foot and set into a base stone, and it should be **big above and small below**. For a peak of two or three pieces: 亦宜上大下小，似有飞舞势, "as if about to fly or dance". **This is the most important shape rule.**
- **Ground and base.** Three patterns appear in the photos:
  - (a) **A low planted mound or bed (花台)**, with 2–5 small stones of 1/5 to 1/3 of the peak's height and tufts of grass or dwarf bamboo at the foot. Examples: 冠云峰, 晚翠峰, the 8-hole peak.
  - (b) **A broad flat base slab, low and wider than the peak's foot, standing in water.** Example: 瑞云峰.
  - (c) **A low rough stone block on a rockery bed.** Example: 玉玲珑.

  Carved wooden stands belong to small desk rocks. **A garden peak never stands on a turned or carved vase-like pedestal.**
- **Against a white wall: this is the moon-gate scene.** 园冶·峭壁山 **[primary]**: 峭壁山者，靠壁理也。藉以粉壁为纸，以石为绘也。理者相石皴纹，仿古人笔意，植黄山松柏、古梅、美竹，收之圆窗，宛然镜游也. In short: build the rock against the wall, with the **white-plastered wall as the paper and the stone as the painting**; choose the stone's wrinkles to imitate the old masters' brushwork; plant pine, old plum or fine bamboo; and **frame it in a round opening, as if travelling through a mirror**. For the moon gate this means:
  - a white wall behind;
  - one peak, set off-centre;
  - one companion plant (bamboo, plum or pine; a banana plant is also traditional beside Taihu rock, see 唐寅's 陶穀赠词图 and the 留园 courtyard photo);
  - grass at the foot.

  This is a preference I inferred, not something a source says: the classic courtyard companions are bamboo, plum, pine and banana, and lotus belongs where the view opens onto a pond.
- **Ji Cheng's warning [primary, 园冶·掇山·厅山]:** do not line up **three tall peaks in a row in front of a hall**; he calls it laughable. Peaks should be offset and of unequal height.
- **Companions.** 园冶·选石 says Taihu stone suits the front of halls and pavilions, or under tall pines and rare flowers. The 冠云峰 group adds stone shoots, flowers, pines and bamboo.

## 5. Rockery (假山) by a pond or under a pavilion, as opposed to a single peak

- **The ideal [primary, 园冶·掇山·池山]:** 就水点其步石，从巅架以飞梁；洞穴潜藏，穿岩径水；风峦飘渺，漏月招云. In short: stepping stones set into the water, a flying bridge from the summit, caves hidden away, a path through rock over water, misty crests that let moonlight leak through and beckon clouds. Ji Cheng calls rockwork by a pond the finest thing in a garden.
- **Skyline rule [primary, 园冶·掇山·峦]:** 峦，山头高峻也，不可齐，亦不可笔架式，或高或低，随至乱掇，不排比为妙. The tops must not be level and must not look like a **brush-rest (笔架)**, a row of evenly spaced bumps. Mix high and low freely and never line them up.
- **What it looks like (Lion Grove, 冠云峰 pond edge, 玉玲珑 pond edge, 虹饮山房):**
  - The mass is **wider than tall**: about 2.5–3.5× for a rockery under a pavilion [photo-est.].
  - It is built from 6–15 visible lumps stacked in **roughly horizontal courses**, each lump overlapping the one below.
  - It has **one main high point**, offset from centre, and 1–3 lower secondary tips. The pavilion sits on or beside the main high part.
  - **One dark cave mouth or grotto** sits at path or water level.
  - **Flat-ish slabs project over the water line, each with a dark undercut shadow under its lip.**
  - Small trees or shrubs grow from the cracks.
  - The stones keep Taihu's rounded, holed character; individual holes are smaller relative to the mass than on a single peak.
- **Example of a pavilion on a rockery:** 冠云亭, a small pavilion on the rockery beside 冠云峰 (Wikipedia). It sits on a low knoll of stacked stones, raised about 0.3–0.6 of its own height above the path [photo-est.].
- **Failure mode:** Shen Fu's 乱堆煤渣, a uniform heap of same-sized lumps with no overall mountain shape.

## 6. How painters and woodblock illustrators draw it

**Woodblock line (素园石谱, 1613; Kansai University copy, vol. 3, viewed).** This is the closest model for an ink line SVG. On the tall peak plate (vol. 3, image 0012):

- **Silhouette:** C-shaped or hooked. A big head curls over to one side, the neck is narrow, and the foot spreads into a lobed base.
- **Outline:** made of **many small convex scallops and notches**, so it reads as a lobed, cloud-like edge with no long smooth curves.
- **Holes:**
  - each is drawn as a **closed oval with a second, partial inner contour**, which shows the thickness of the rim;
  - the inside is darker;
  - one big hole sits mid-body and 2–3 smaller "eyes" sit near it.
- **Interior texture:** short, slightly curved parallel strokes like ripples, following each bulge, grouped on the shadow sides only.
- **White:** about **60–70% of the stone is bare paper**.

A second plate (何君石, image 0008) shows the 弹子窝 convention: a dark mass covered in small white rings, like bubbles.

**Painting.**
- **宋徽宗 祥龙石图 (Palace Museum, viewed):** top-heavy, a broad head over a narrower stem, lateral arms reaching out, and see-through holes left as **light shapes where the background shows**. The surface is stippled with small round pits (弹子窝).
- **唐寅 陶穀赠词图 (viewed):** a garden Taihu mass made of interlocking rounded lobes, **darker wash in every concavity and undercut, lit convex ridges left light**, and **banana plants growing right beside it**.
- **Mustard Seed Garden Manual page (Met, viewed, rock after Su Shi):** a firm outline, very few interior strokes, a handful of dark **moss dots (苔点)** along the edges and the base, and lots of white.

**Named texture strokes (via modern summaries of 芥子园画传 and 郑绩's list of sixteen 皴).**
- **弹涡皴:** a whirlpool shape. The brush is tilted slightly and turned in small circles, 多作石眼，如水泡然 (many stone eyes, like bubbles). One writer says it is the hallmark of Lake Tai water stones (also called "fish-scale pattern").
- **骷髅皴:** skull-like hollows, used for pierced, see-through stones. It occasionally appears in 王蒙, 沈周 and 文徵明.
- **卷云皴:** curling cloud strokes, used for rounded masses.

**General order and rules for painting rocks (teaching articles):**
- The order is 勾 outline → 皴 texture → 擦 dry-brush rub → 染 wash → 点 dots.
- The outline should 时直时曲、时凹时凸、时方时圆: straight then curved, hollow then bulging, square then round, with 顿挫 (press-and-turn accents) rather than one even line.
- Texture follows the structure, never across it.
- **The wash must be lighter than the texture strokes** (染淡于皴), or the rock goes stiff.
- In 没骨 (boneless) 湖石, the painter blocks in the mass, leaves white, then drops **holes of different sizes in deep ink**.

## 7. Worked silhouettes for a 150–300 px drawing (my construction from the photos and rules above, not a trace)

### A. Lean peak (冠云峰 / 晚翠峰 type), for inside the moon gate

The box is 100 wide × 240 tall, with y = 0 at the top and the ground line at y = 238. The proportion is about **3.2:1**. The bands narrow from the top down:

| Band | Width | Share of head |
|---|---|---|
| Head (y ≈ 28) | about 74 | 100% |
| Arm level (y ≈ 128) | about 62 | head is about 19% wider |
| Lower body (y ≈ 170–200) | about 54 | |
| Foot (y = 238) | about 56 | |
| Waist (y ≈ 92–100) | about 40 | 54% |

The head must stay clearly the widest band (上大下小).

**Left edge, crown to foot:**
1. **Crown.** Start at the crown tip (62,4). Sweep left over the hook: (40,2) → (20,12) → beak at (12,28), pointing down-left. End at (16,40) with a small notch back in to (24,36).
2. **Under-hook hollow.** Concave in to (34,52), out to a bulge at (26,70), in to the waist notch at (36,92).
3. **Side arm.** A short stub: (22,118) → (20,128) → (28,134), then in to (34,142).
4. **Lower body.** Barely swelling: (30,170) → (34,200), then easing to the foot: (30,226) → (28,238).

**Right edge, crown to foot:** (62,4) → (78,10) → shoulder at (86,26) → notch (82,44) → bulge (88,62) → (80,84) → **waist (76,100)** → bulge (82,124) → notch (78,150) → (84,176) → (80,204) → (82,228) → foot (84,238).

**Line weight:**
- 1.6–2.2 px on the shadow side (say the right) and along the foot;
- 0.8–1.2 px on the lit left and the top;
- break the line in 2–3 places on the lit edges.

**Holes:**
- H1, a large **through-hole in the head**: oval centred (46,26), about 16×11, tilted −20°. Show the background inside it, and draw a partial inner rim line on its lower-right.
- H2, medium: a teardrop at (60,64), about 9×14, vertical, blind, so fill it dark.
- H3, medium: a kidney-shaped hollow under the arm at (40,122), about 10×7, dark.
- H4: a slot at (66,150), about 4×10.
- Pits: 4–6 tiny ovals of 3–5 px at (52,90), (70,40), (30,180), (58,196), (48,214).
- No two holes the same size or orientation.

**Texture:**
- 3–5 long, slightly wavy, broken vertical grooves running from about y 45 to 220, concentrated on the shadow half;
- short arcs hugging the rims of H1 and H2;
- two tiny clusters of 3–5 rings (r 1–2 px) at about (64,110) and (40,168).

**Ground:**
- the foot sinks into a low mound line;
- 2–3 small rocks, 25–60 px wide and 15–40 px tall, on either side;
- a few grass-blade strokes;
- 3–6 dark moss dots at the foot and in the waist crease.

### B. Perforated peak (玉玲珑 / 瑞云峰 type)

- **Proportion:** about 1.6:1 to 2:1. For example 110 wide × 190 tall.
- **Mass:** widest at the upper shoulders (y about 40–70). It pinches to a neck of about 45–50% of the maximum width just above the base.
- **Base:** a broad, low, flat stone about 1.3× the neck width and 1/8 of the height. In water, draw a dark undercut line under the slab.
- **Holes:** 15–30 holes in a strict size hierarchy: 2 large (see-through), 5–8 medium, the rest small. They are crowded in the upper 2/3, and some merge into one another (洞中有洞).

### C. Pond-side rock among the lotus (left of the pavilion scene)

- **Shape:** a low companion stone, 1:1 to 1.5:1 (e.g. 70 × 90), leaning, with a slight overhang on the water side.
- **Holes:** one medium hole and 2–3 pits.
- **Base:** a flat slab at the water line with a dark undercut.
- **Lotus:** the leaves overlap the rock's foot, not its head.

### D. Rockery under the pavilion

- **Size:** about 260 × 90 for a pavilion about 70 wide.
- **Lumps:** 6–12 overlapping lumps in 2–3 rough horizontal courses.
- **Skyline:** the top line rises to one main high point under or just beside the pavilion. Add one lower secondary tip at 0.6–0.7 of the main height, on the other side, and keep the tips at uneven spacing.
- **Cave:** one dark cave mouth, 15–25 px, near the bottom.
- **Water edge:** 2–3 flat slabs lipping over the water, each with a dark undercut.
- **Steps:** a step path of 3–4 flat stones climbing to the pavilion.
- **Plants:** one small tree or shrub growing from a crack.

## 8. Tone, to answer the "too saturated" note

- **Stone:** paper white (lit planes), **pale cool neutral grey** wash for the main form (about 10–25% ink), **darkest grey** in undercuts and blind holes (about 45–65%). Keep the hue neutral or very slightly blue-grey, matching the 青灰 / 青黑 of the sources. **No brown, ochre, green or blue tint on the rock.**
- **Through-holes:** show whatever lies behind. In the moon gate that is the white wall or the pale dusk sky; do not fill them black.
- **Line and texture:** the line is dark warm-grey ink. Texture strokes are lighter than the outline. Washes are lighter than texture strokes (染淡于皴).
- **Moss dots:** dark ink, not green.

## 9. What a naive drawing gets wrong

- **A potato or blob with round holes.** It is bottom-heavy or centred, its outline is smooth, and its holes are circles. Real peaks are 上大下小, hook-headed and lobed.
- **Swiss cheese.** Evenly spaced holes of one size cover the stone and are all black. Real holes have one big, a few medium and many small, are clustered high, and some are see-through.
- **A symmetric vase or flame.** A bilateral silhouette, or one with its widest point at the bottom. Real peaks lean or hook to one side.
- **A squat lump** (about 1:1) used as the feature peak. Feature peaks are 2:1 to 4:1. Only companions and pond-edge stones are squat.
- **A single-weight smooth outline** that reads as clip-art or a cloud. Real outlines are many-lobed, with 顿挫 accents and gaps.
- **Sharp angular facets or layered strata,** which read as yellowstone, granite or Ying stone. Taihu is rounded and water-worn.
- **Coloured stone,** whether sandstone brown, mossy green or blue. Taihu is grey on white.
- **A rock floating on the ground line or sitting on a pedestal.** Real peaks rise from a planted mound or a flat base slab with small stones at the foot.
- **A rockery drawn as a 笔架 row of equal bumps, or a cinder heap.** Real rockeries have one main mass, horizontal courses, a cave and an uneven skyline.
- **Three equal peaks lined up symmetrically** in a courtyard (园冶 calls this laughable).

## Draw it like this

1. Make it top-heavy (上大下小): the widest part sits in the upper third, the waist is about half the head's width, and the foot flares slightly.
2. Pick the proportion by role: a feature peak is 3–4:1 (lean) or about 2:1 (perforated, like 玉玲珑, or 瑞云峰 at 1.6:1 with its base); a pond-side companion is 1:1–1.5:1; a rockery is 2.5–3.5 times wider than it is tall.
3. Hook or lean the head to one side so it overhangs the foot by 15–30% of the height; never centre it like a vase.
4. Build the outline from 8–15 unequal lobes with notches between them. Add 2–3 sharp turns, use a heavier line on the shadow side and at the foot, and let the line thin and break along the lit top.
5. Place one big hole off-centre in the upper third, plus 2–4 medium holes and 4–8 small pits, all of different sizes and shapes (oval, teardrop, kidney, slot). The perforated type gets 15–30, crowded high.
6. Draw each hole as a closed loop with a partial inner rim line. Fill blind holes with the darkest wash, and let see-through holes show the wall or sky behind.
7. Texture sparingly along the form: long vertical grooves on lean peaks, short curved hatching on the shadow sides of bulges, and 2–3 tiny clusters of rings (弹子窝). Leave 50–70% of the stone as bare paper.
8. Wash in cool neutral grey only: paper white on lit planes, pale grey for the body, darkest in undercuts. Keep washes lighter than the texture strokes, and give the stone no colour.
9. Seat the peak in a low planted mound with 2–5 small stones and grass tufts at its foot, or on a broad flat base slab in water. Never use a pedestal, and never let it float.
10. In the moon gate, put one peak off-centre against the white wall with one plant beside it (bamboo, plum, pine or banana), and let its head rise above the wall coping if there is room (园冶: 粉壁为纸，以石为绘……收之圆窗).
11. Under the pavilion, stack 6–12 lumps in rough horizontal courses: one main high point, lower tips at uneven heights, a dark cave mouth low down, and flat slabs lipping over the water with dark undercuts.
12. Finish with 3–6 dark ink moss dots at the foot and in the creases, and stop there.

## Tell-tale mistakes (and the fix)

- **A potato or blob silhouette (bottom-heavy, smooth, centred).** Fix: invert the weight so the head is the biggest mass, add a pinched waist, and hook the head to one side.
- **Swiss cheese (many same-size round holes spread evenly).** Fix: use one large off-centre hole high up, a few medium and several small, make every shape different, and let some merge.
- **All holes filled solid black, like stickers.** Fix: let through-holes show the background, fill only blind holes dark, and give each hole an inner rim line.
- **A symmetric vase or flame shape, or its widest point at the bottom.** Fix: break the symmetry with a lean or hook of 15–30% of the height, one side arm, and notches at different heights on the two sides.
- **A squat lump used as the main peak.** Fix: stretch it to 2:1–4:1 for the feature peak, and keep squat stones as companions only.
- **A smooth, even-weight outline that reads as a cloud or clip-art.** Fix: build it from many small scallops with notches and 2–3 sharp turns, vary the line weight, and leave gaps on the lit edges.
- **Sharp crystal facets or layered strata (reads as yellowstone, granite or Ying stone).** Fix: round every edge into water-worn lobes and rims, and use vertical flutes and pits instead of strata.
- **Tinted stone (brown, green, blue).** Fix: use grey ink on paper white only, light washes, and dark only in hollows.
- **A rock floating above the ground line or set on a vase-like pedestal.** Fix: sink the foot into a planted mound with small stones and grass, or set it on a broad flat base slab in water.
- **A rockery drawn as a row of equal bumps (笔架) or a heap of same-size boulders (Shen Fu's "coal-cinder heap").** Fix: give it one main mass with lower secondary tips at uneven spacing, horizontal courses, a cave mouth, and slabs overhanging the water.
- **Several equal peaks lined up symmetrically in front of a building (园冶 calls this laughable).** Fix: use one off-centre peak per frame, or unequal peaks at staggered depths. Prefer courtyard companions (bamboo, plum, pine, banana) beside a walled-courtyard peak; lotus suits the pond scene (my inference, not a source rule).

## Confidence and gaps

- **High:** the shape rules (top-heavy, hooked head, lobed outline, size hierarchy of holes, planted mound or flat base, white-wall framing, rockery skyline). They rest on 园冶 read directly, on twelve photographs of named Suzhou and Jiangnan peaks, and on 1613 woodcuts and Song–Ming paintings I looked at myself.
- **Medium:** the exact ratios. Only 瑞云峰, 玉玲珑 and the Cleveland stone have published widths. 冠云峰's 6.5 m comes from travel and encyclopaedia sources, not the garden bureau, and its width is my photo estimate.
- **Not confirmed:**
  - dimensions or a description of 岫云峰;
  - anything at all about 朵云峰;
  - 李渔's 闲情偶寄 in the original (only modern quotations);
  - the 芥子园画传 rock pages themselves (only modern summaries of its 弹涡皴 / 骷髅皴 text);
  - a 素园石谱 plate that is explicitly labelled 太湖石 (the plates I saw are of other named stones drawn in the same convention).
